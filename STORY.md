# Proposed pitch arc — not locked

Working title: **LOW ORBIT, HIGH STATUS**

Alternate names: **Mile High Club**, **Orbit Rewards**, **SkyMiles Per Gallon**. Team decides.

| Slide | What they see | What we say / reveal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Vision | Beautiful orbital render above an aggressively ordinary truck stop | “The future of compute is orbital. The future of distribution is Exit 147.” |
| 2. Problem | An unnecessarily serious photo of a shower queue | Truckers need reliable services. We have diagnosed a catastrophic shortage of elite status. |
| 3. Architecture | Space → satellite dish → truck stop → taquito | Explain the expensive system straight-faced. “We solve the last mile by adding 250 miles vertically.” |
| 4. Unfair advantage | Will as a superhero; nearly no other content | “Will can get it into orbit. Nobody can explain why it belongs there.” |
| 5. Product | Four membership cards: Diesel / Gold / Platinum / Divorced | Priority shower access, lounge taquitos, airline miles, deeply arbitrary qualification rules. |
| 6. Business model | Giant loyalty-card graphic; satellite shrinks to a footnote | **“The satellites are a customer acquisition cost.”** Pause. Explain points sales, fees, and status anxiety with absurd confidence. |
| 7. Why Starship | An LP badge visually transforms into a loyalty card | “LP stands for Loyalty Points.” “Your capital is locked up for ten years. Your shower credits expire Friday.” |
| 8. Ask | $69M seed; 1% engineering, 99% convincing people they're Platinum | “Our exit strategy? Exit 147.” Stop. |

All numbers are invented comedy props. The designer can vary them; do not mix them with researched market data as if they were real projections.

## Delivery

Start controlled and credible. Let the slides expose the stupidity. Pause after the reveal. Finish on a short line rather than explaining the joke.
